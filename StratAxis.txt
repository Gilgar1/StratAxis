StratAxis
A Data Engineering and Analytics Platform for Real-Estate Market Intelligence in Cameroon

1. Overview
StratAxis is a data analytics and advisory platform designed to address the absence of reliable, centralized, and structured real-estate data in Cameroon, starting with Yaoundé and Douala.
The platform combines:
•	Automated data collection
•	Data cleaning and validation
•	Statistical analysis and machine-learning forecasting
•	Human-in-the-loop consulting
to transform fragmented, unreliable real-estate information into actionable market intelligence for investors, developers, institutions, and serious buyers.
StratAxis is not a listing platform and not an agent marketplace.
It is a market intelligence and decision-support system.

2. Problem Statement
In Cameroon:
•	There is no official Multiple Listing Service (MLS).

o	Fragmented across platforms (agents, WhatsApp, Facebook, classified sites)

•	Government and institutional data exists mostly in:
o	PDFs

As a result:

Problem Statement
Access to reliable, structured, and timely real-estate market data is a critical prerequisite for informed investment, urban planning, and economic decision-making. In many developed markets, this need is addressed through centralized systems such as Multiple Listing Services (MLS), public land registries, and regularly published housing indices. However, in Cameroon—particularly in its two major urban centers, Yaoundé and Douala—such systems are either non-existent, inaccessible, or severely fragmented.
Real-estate information in Cameroon is currently dispersed across informal and unreliable sources, including individual agents, classified websites, social media platforms, and word-of-mouth networks. Property listings often suffer from duplicated entries, inconsistent pricing, exaggerated valuations, missing metadata (such as location size and zoning), and lack of historical records. In addition, relevant institutional and regulatory data—such as land policies, zoning updates, and urban development plans—are frequently published in PDF or scanned formats, making them difficult to extract, analyze, and integrate into data-driven systems.
As a result of this fragmentation and opacity, key stakeholders—including investors, developers, diaspora buyers, NGOs, and researchers—are forced to make high-stakes decisions under significant uncertainty. This leads to inefficient capital allocation, increased exposure to fraud and speculation, distorted market perceptions, and reduced confidence in the real-estate sector as a whole. While some macroeconomic and statistical data exists through government or regional institutions, it is rarely contextualized, combined with market-level data, or translated into actionable insights relevant to real-estate decision-making.
From a software engineering perspective, this problem is compounded by the technical challenges of working with unreliable, heterogeneous, and incomplete data in an emerging market context. Traditional data pipelines assume clean, structured inputs, whereas the Cameroonian real-estate data environment is characterized by noise, inconsistency, and uncertainty. There is therefore a need for systems that not only collect data, but also clean, validate, normalize, and quantify confidence in that data, while remaining transparent about limitations.
This project proposes StratAxis, a data engineering and analytics platform designed to address these challenges by systematically extracting real-estate data from multiple public and semi-public sources, processing it through a robust ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) pipeline, and applying statistical and machine-learning techniques to analyze trends and generate forecasts. The platform focuses on Yaoundé and Douala as an initial scope, prioritizing depth, data integrity, and methodological rigor over broad but shallow coverage.
By emphasizing pipeline robustness, data validation, confidence scoring, and honest uncertainty handling—rather than absolute prediction accuracy—StratAxis aims to demonstrate how software engineering and applied machine learning can be used to reduce information asymmetry in data-poor environments. The core problem addressed by this project is therefore not merely the absence of data, but the absence of trustworthy, structured, and decision-oriented real-estate intelligence in Cameroon.




















Project Objectives
1. General Objective
The general objective of this project is to design and implement StratAxis, a data engineering and analytics platform that systematically collects, cleans, validates, and analyzes fragmented real-estate data in Cameroon, with a focus on Yaoundé and Douala, in order to generate structured market intelligence and support data-driven decision-making in an emerging market context.

2. Specific Technical Objectives
The specific technical objectives of this project are to:
1.	Design and implement an automated data ingestion system capable of extracting real-estate and institutional data from heterogeneous sources, including websites and PDF-based documents, using web scraping and Optical Character Recognition (OCR) techniques.
2.	Develop a robust ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) pipeline to clean, normalize, and structure raw data by:
o	Removing duplicate and inconsistent records,
o	Standardizing prices, units, and locations,
o	Handling missing and noisy data appropriately.
3.	Implement data validation and integrity mechanisms, including outlier detection, cross-source verification, and confidence scoring, to quantify the reliability of collected data rather than assuming absolute accuracy.
4.	Store and manage processed data efficiently using a structured database design that supports querying, aggregation, and historical analysis.
5.	Apply statistical and machine learning techniques to analyze historical trends and generate real-estate price forecasts, while explicitly measuring and reporting model performance using standard evaluation metrics.
6.	Develop interactive data visualizations and dashboards that allow users to explore real-estate trends by location, time period, and property characteristics.
7.	Implement a secure role-based access control (RBAC) system that differentiates between public users and premium clients, ensuring controlled access to advanced features and consultation services.
8.	Deploy the platform as a live web application, demonstrating end-to-end system integration, scalability considerations, and basic security practices such as authentication and encrypted communication.

3. Specific Business and Functional Objectives
The specific business and functional objectives of this project are to:
1.	Reduce information asymmetry in the Cameroonian real-estate market by providing free access to structured market data and visual analytics for Yaoundé and Douala.
2.	Provide decision-support insights for investors, developers, and institutions through data-driven analysis rather than informal or speculative sources.
3.	Establish a sustainable monetization model based on paid consultations and customized analytical reports, rather than selling raw data.
4.	Demonstrate real-world applicability by deploying the platform publicly and onboarding initial users before project defense.
5.	Build credibility and trust through transparent methodology, documented data limitations, and consistent analytical outputs.









Scope and Limitations
1. Scope of the Project
This project focuses on the design, implementation, and evaluation of StratAxis, a data engineering and analytics platform for real-estate market intelligence in Cameroon. The scope of the project is deliberately constrained to ensure depth, technical rigor, and successful implementation within the time and resource limitations of a final-year Bachelor of Science in Software Engineering project.
1.1 Geographical Scope
The project is limited to Yaoundé and Douala, Cameroon’s two largest urban centers. These cities were selected due to their high real-estate activity, data availability, and economic significance. The system architecture, however, is designed to allow future geographical expansion.
1.2 Sectoral Scope
The project focuses exclusively on the real-estate sector. Data collection, analysis, and modeling are limited to real-estate-related metrics such as land prices, rental prices, property characteristics, and location-based trends. Other economic sectors are outside the scope of this implementation.
1.3 Data Scope
StratAxis processes data obtained from:
•	Publicly accessible real-estate listing platforms,
•	Public and semi-public institutional publications,
•	PDF and scanned documents extracted using OCR.
The system emphasizes historical and aggregated data analysis rather than real-time transaction data. Data is treated as probabilistic and is accompanied by confidence indicators.
1.4 Functional Scope
Within the defined scope, the system provides:
•	Automated data ingestion through web scraping and OCR,
•	Data cleaning, normalization, and validation,
•	Descriptive analytics and trend visualization,
•	Predictive modeling for price trend estimation,
•	Role-based access control distinguishing free users from premium clients,
•	A publicly accessible web interface with dashboards and a blog section.
1.5 Temporal Scope
The project analyzes historical data collected during the development period and does not guarantee continuous long-term data updates beyond the project timeline.

2. Limitations of the Project
The following limitations are acknowledged and explicitly stated to ensure transparency and academic integrity:
2.1 Data Quality Limitations
The accuracy of the system’s outputs is inherently constrained by the quality of available data. Real-estate listings in Cameroon are often inconsistent, duplicated, or inflated. While the system applies validation and confidence scoring techniques, it cannot fully eliminate inaccuracies present in source data.
2.2 Prediction and Modeling Limitations
The predictive models implemented in this project are intended to identify general trends and directional patterns, not to provide precise price predictions or financial guarantees. Model performance is limited by dataset size, feature availability, and historical coverage.
2.3 Geographical and Sectoral Limitations
The system does not cover rural areas, secondary cities, or other economic sectors such as agriculture, manufacturing, or financial markets. Insights generated by StratAxis should therefore not be generalized beyond the defined geographical and sectoral scope.
2.4 Legal and Access Limitations
Data collection is restricted to publicly accessible sources. The system does not include proprietary or confidential data from government agencies or private institutions unless such data is legally and explicitly provided.
2.5 Resource and Time Constraints
As an academic project, StratAxis is developed within a limited timeframe and without significant financial resources. Consequently, large-scale data acquisition, advanced infrastructure optimization, and long-term system maintenance are outside the scope of this implementation.
2.6 Advisory Limitations
StratAxis does not provide legal, financial, or investment advice. All analytical outputs are intended solely for informational and decision-support purposes.




















1. Methodology
1.1 Research and Development Approach
This project adopts a design-and-implementation methodology, combining principles from software engineering, data engineering, and applied machine learning. The approach is primarily experimental and iterative, focusing on building, testing, and evaluating a working system rather than purely theoretical analysis.
Given the real-world constraints of unreliable and fragmented data in an emerging market context, the methodology emphasizes:
•	Incremental development,
•	Empirical evaluation of data quality,
•	Transparency in assumptions and limitations.

1.2 Data Collection Methodology
1.2.1 Web Scraping
Automated web scrapers are developed to extract real-estate listings and related information from publicly accessible online platforms. These scrapers are scheduled to run at defined intervals and are designed to:
•	Respect rate limits,
•	Handle structural changes in source websites,
•	Log extraction errors and metadata such as timestamps and source identifiers.
1.2.2 Document Processing and OCR
Institutional and regulatory data that is only available in PDF or scanned formats is processed using Optical Character Recognition (OCR). Extracted text is further parsed and structured using rule-based techniques to identify relevant entities such as locations, dates, and policy references.
1.2.3 Data Storage (Raw Layer)
All collected data is initially stored in a raw, unmodified form to preserve data provenance and allow traceability during later stages of processing.

1.3 Data Cleaning and Transformation
An ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) pipeline is implemented to process raw data into a structured and analyzable format.
Key transformation steps include:
•	Removal of duplicate listings using heuristic and rule-based matching,
•	Normalization of prices into a single currency (FCFA) and standardized units (price per square meter),
•	Standardization of geographical identifiers (city, district, neighborhood),
•	Handling of missing values through imputation or record exclusion, depending on severity.
The cleaned data is stored separately from raw data to maintain a clear separation between ingestion and processing stages.

1.4 Data Validation and Integrity Assurance
Given the unreliable nature of source data, the system incorporates multiple validation mechanisms:
•	Outlier detection to identify abnormal price values,
•	Cross-source comparison to verify consistency between independent data sources,
•	Temporal validation to ensure logical price progression over time.
Each data record is assigned a confidence score reflecting its estimated reliability. This score is later used to weight analytical and predictive outputs.

1.5 Analytical and Predictive Modeling
1.5.1 Descriptive Analytics
Statistical techniques are applied to generate summary metrics such as:
•	Median and average prices by location,
•	Price distributions,
•	Temporal trends.
1.5.2 Predictive Modeling
Machine learning models are trained on historical data to estimate future price trends. Multiple models are evaluated, including:
•	Linear regression models,
•	Tree-based regression models,
•	Time-series forecasting models.
Model performance is evaluated using standard metrics such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). The selected model is chosen based on empirical performance rather than complexity.
Importantly, predictive outputs are presented with confidence intervals, emphasizing uncertainty rather than absolute predictions.

1.6 System Implementation and Deployment
The system is implemented as a web-based application with a modular architecture. After development and testing, the platform is deployed to a live hosting environment with:
•	Secure user authentication,
•	Encrypted communication (HTTPS),
•	Logging and monitoring.

1.7 Evaluation Strategy
The system is evaluated based on:
•	Correctness and robustness of the data pipeline,
•	Performance of predictive models,
•	Usability and accessibility of dashboards,
•	Ability to handle incomplete and noisy data.

2. System Architecture Description
2.1 Architectural Overview
StratAxis is built using a layered, modular architecture designed to support scalability, maintainability, and clear separation of concerns. The system consists of five primary layers:
1.	Data Ingestion Layer
2.	Data Processing and Storage Layer
3.	Analytics and Machine Learning Layer
4.	Application and API Layer
5.	Presentation Layer

2.2 Data Ingestion Layer
This layer is responsible for collecting data from external sources. It includes:
•	Web scraping services for online listings,
•	OCR services for PDF and scanned documents,
•	Schedulers to control ingestion frequency.
All ingested data is accompanied by metadata such as source, timestamp, and extraction method.

2.3 Data Processing and Storage Layer
2.3.1 Raw Data Store
Raw, unprocessed data is stored to ensure traceability and reproducibility.
2.3.2 Processed Data Store
Cleaned and validated data is stored in a structured database optimized for querying and aggregation.
The separation of raw and processed data ensures that transformations can be audited and re-run if necessary.

2.4 Analytics and Machine Learning Layer
This layer performs:
•	Statistical analysis,
•	Feature engineering,
•	Model training and evaluation,
•	Forecast generation.
Models are versioned, and their performance metrics are stored to allow comparison and transparency.

2.5 Application and API Layer
The application layer exposes system functionality through secure APIs. It handles:
•	User authentication and authorization,
•	Role-based access control (RBAC),
•	Data access requests,
•	Consultation booking workflows.
This layer enforces business logic and security policies.

2.6 Presentation Layer
The presentation layer provides the user-facing interface, including:
•	Interactive dashboards and charts,
•	Filters for location and time range,
•	Public blog pages,
•	Consultation request forms.
The interface is designed to prioritize clarity and usability, allowing non-technical users to interpret data effectively.

2.7 Security and Reliability Considerations
Security measures include:
•	Encrypted data transmission,
•	Authentication and access control,
•	Activity logging.
Reliability considerations include:
•	Error handling in data ingestion,
•	Backup of critical datasets,
•	Monitoring of system uptime.

1. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
FR-1: User Registration and Authentication
•	The system shall allow users to create an account using email and password.
•	The system shall authenticate users using secure credentials.
•	The system shall assign a role (FREE, PREMIUM, ADMIN) to each user.
FR-2: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
•	The system shall restrict access to features based on user roles.
•	Free users shall access public dashboards and blog content only.
•	Premium users shall access advanced analytics and consultation booking.
•	Admin users shall manage data sources and monitor system operations.
FR-3: Data Ingestion (Web Scraping)
•	The system shall automatically extract real-estate listings from publicly accessible websites.
•	The system shall store scraped data with metadata (source, timestamp).
•	The system shall log scraping failures and errors.
FR-4: Document Processing and OCR
•	The system shall extract text from PDF and scanned documents using OCR.
•	The system shall identify relevant real-estate or policy information from extracted text.
FR-5: Data Cleaning and Normalization
•	The system shall remove duplicate records.
•	The system shall normalize prices into a single currency (FCFA).
•	The system shall standardize units (price per square meter).
•	The system shall standardize location names.
FR-6: Data Validation and Confidence Scoring
•	The system shall detect and flag outliers.
•	The system shall cross-validate data across multiple sources.
•	The system shall assign a confidence score to each processed record.
FR-7: Data Storage
•	The system shall store raw data separately from processed data.
•	The system shall maintain historical records for trend analysis.
FR-8: Analytics and Visualization
•	The system shall generate descriptive statistics (median, trends).
•	The system shall display analytics using charts and maps.
•	The system shall allow filtering by quarter, city, location, and time range.
FR-9: Predictive Modeling
•	The system shall train machine learning models on historical data.
•	The system shall generate price trend forecasts with confidence intervals.
•	The system shall store prediction results for analysis.
FR-10: Consultation Booking
•	The system shall allow premium users to request consultations.
•	The system shall store consultation requests and user details.
FR-11: Blog Management
•	The system shall display blog articles to all users.
•	The system shall allow administrators to manage blog content.

2. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
2.1 Performance Requirements
•	The system shall load dashboard pages within <=3 seconds under normal conditions.
•	Data ingestion jobs shall complete within scheduled execution windows.
2.2 Security Requirements
•	The system shall encrypt sensitive data in transit using HTTPS.
•	The system shall store passwords using secure hashing mechanisms.
•	Unauthorized users shall not access premium features.
2.3 Reliability Requirements
•	The system shall log system errors and failures.
•	The system shall recover gracefully from scraper or OCR failures.
2.4 Availability Requirements
•	The system shall be available at least 95% uptime during the project period.
2.5 Maintainability Requirements
•	The system shall use modular components for ingestion, processing, and analytics.
•	Code shall follow standard naming and documentation conventions.
2.6 Scalability Requirements
•	The architecture shall allow the addition of new data sources without redesign.
•	The system shall support future expansion to additional cities.
2.7 Usability Requirements
•	The user interface shall be intuitive and usable by non-technical users.
•	Data visualizations shall be clearly labeled and easy to interpret.
